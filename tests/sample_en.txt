Apple's M5 MacBook Pro Is the First Real Reason to Upgrade Since 2021

I have used the same 14-inch MacBook Pro for four years. Last week Apple gave me a reason to finally replace it.

Apple announced the M5 MacBook Pro on Wednesday, and the numbers are unusual. The company claims 4x faster AI compute than M4 thanks to a redesigned Neural Accelerator sitting inside every GPU core. In my two days of testing, a 7B parameter local model generated tokens roughly 3.2x faster than on my M4 Pro machine.

What actually changed
The M5 chip moves to a third-generation 3nm process. Apple says single-thread CPU performance is up 15 percent, and GPU graphics performance up 45 percent. Memory bandwidth jumps to 153 GB/s, a 28 percent increase.

Battery life stays at 24 hours for video playback. Price starts at 1,599 dollars for the 14-inch model with 16GB unified memory, which is the same price as the M4 launch price last year.

Why this matters for people who edit video
Final Cut Pro exports of a 10-minute 4K ProRes timeline finished in 2 minutes 11 seconds on the M5, against 3 minutes 40 seconds on the M4. That is the kind of gap that changes a working day.

Should you upgrade?
If you bought an M4 machine last year, no. If you are still on M1 or Intel, this is the clearest jump in four years. Apple starts shipping on October 22.
